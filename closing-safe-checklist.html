Is your email closing-safe? A 10-point checklist

Ten questions that show whether your office's email is protected against wire fraud, with why each one matters and how to check it.

By Ed Dupuy, Sealbridge Security · Updated October 20, 2026

How to use it

Check the box for each statement that is true for your office today, then count your checks. 9 or 10 means you're in good shape. 6 to 8 means you have gaps attackers look for. 5 or fewer means fix this before your next closing.

The 10 questions

  1. Why it matters: A stolen password alone is the most common way into a mailbox. Multi-factor sign-in stops most of those attempts.

    How to check: In Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, check the multi-factor status of every account, not just the ones people use daily.

  2. Why it matters: Attackers add rules that copy closing emails to themselves or hide replies. The rule keeps working after the password is changed.

    How to check: See how to find hidden forwarding rules in Microsoft 365.

  3. Why it matters: A buyer who knows what normal looks like is far more likely to call before wiring.

    How to check: Check your opening package and engagement letters for the notice.

  4. Why it matters: A scammer controls every phone number in a fake email.

    How to check: See the 7-step verification procedure.

  5. Why it matters: Without it, anyone can send email that shows your exact address in the From line.

    How to check: Run the free spoofing check on your domain.

  6. Why it matters: Lookalike domains are how scammers send convincing fake instructions without breaking into anything.

    How to check: The free spoofing check lists registered lookalikes of your domain.

  7. Why it matters: Old accounts are rarely watched, so a takeover can go unnoticed for months.

    How to check: Compare your list of active email accounts with your current staff and agents.

  8. Why it matters: Most attacks start with one convincing email to one busy person.

    How to check: Check training dates for closers, processors and front-desk staff.

  9. Why it matters: Funds are much easier to freeze in the first hours than after a day or two.

    How to check: Post the bank fraud line and the steps from the guide where closers can see them.

  10. Why it matters: Many errors-and-omissions policies exclude stolen wires, and social-engineering limits are often far below a typical closing.

    How to check: Ask your agent for your social-engineering or funds-transfer-fraud limit in writing.
